Raspberry Pi Thermal Performance Research

Designed and conducted an experimental investigation evaluating whether 3D-printed cases could improve the thermal performance of a Raspberry Pi running AI workloads.

Overview

For my senior capstone project in high school, I really wanted to do something engineering-related rather than the traditional psychology route.

A large issue in today's AI-driven society is the heat produced by data centers. I wondered, is there a way to get edge devices to perform efficiently in an area where water or forced-air cooling is not possible?

By combining a Raspberry Pi and thermally conductive 3D printing filament (carbon-fiber filled), I planned to see whether a case could prevent the Raspberry Pi from throttling under load, while also maintaining a protective casing and a stylish look.

Implementation

My project was broken down into a couple of parts.

  1. Researching previous work on thermal management to ensure my project was unique and feasible.
  2. Designing and 3D printing cases of different colors and materials.
  3. Automation using a Python script to both collect temperature data, graph it, and conduct quantitative analysis.
  4. Written and oral presentation as a final product.

Testing & results

To measure my results, I timed how long it would take for the Raspberry Pi to reach a "throttled" state (>80C) for each subject, repeating trials for precision.

According to the ANOVA analysis, using the carbon-fiber case actually did improve the heat transfer from the Raspberry Pi to the surrounding air compared to an open-air subject and a normal PLA subject.

What I learned

I really loved doing this project, and I got a lot of experience of how research is professional done. Also, I got hands-on experience in 3D printer design and material science!
