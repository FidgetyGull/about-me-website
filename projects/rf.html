Amateur Radio

Amateur ("Ham") radio is a non-commercial communication and technical hobby that lets licensed operators talk locally, globally, and even into space without using cell towers, the internet, or even a lot of electricity.

  • My current license is Technician. This gives me basic access to many frequencies that I can freely talk on, provided I follow amateur radio guidelines. The three licenses are Technician, General, and Extra, with each license having harder and harder exams.
  • Every ham operator has a callsign, just like an airplane has a tail number. This unique set of letters identifies you in a worldwide database by the FCC.
  • Antennas must be tuned to match the electrical impedance of your transmitter. If the impedance does not match, part of the radio frequencies will bounce around the cable rather than being transmitted into the air, which harms the radio transmitter.
  • Impedance is measured in ohms, and can be done using a Standard Wave Ratio meter. To tune, you can trim the antenna wires. The above antenna was tuned by me.

SDR

Software Defined Radio (SDR) is a communication system where traditional hardware components are replaced with digital chips controlled by software. It allows you to extend what your antenna can do, without expensive modulators, filters, mixers, etc.

With a cheap Realtek ADC chip-based SDR, I can:

  1. Listen to single frequencies, like air traffic control, FM/AM radio, and standard voice channels.
  2. Listen to trunked frequencies, which are digital voice channels with frequencies assigned by a central computer.
  3. Track where satellites are!
